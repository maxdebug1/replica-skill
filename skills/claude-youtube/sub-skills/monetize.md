# monetize: turn attention into income without spending trust

Lays out which revenue streams fit this channel now, what each needs, and
the next step for each. Ads are usually the smallest slice for small and
mid-sized channels; the plan should not wait on them.

Read: `references/monetization-guide.md` in full and the RPM section of
`references/analytics-guide.md`.

## Inputs

- Stage: subscribers, last-365-day public watch hours, last-90-day Shorts
  views `[Studio]`, country (YPP and features vary by country).
- Audience: who they are, what they buy, what they ask the creator for in
  comments.
- What the creator would happily sell or recommend, and what they will not.
- Current income streams and amounts, if they want to share them `[user]`.

## Steps

1. **Eligibility.** Where are they against the YouTube Partner Program
   tiers in the reference? What exactly is missing and how long at the
   current pace `[est]`, with the arithmetic shown.
2. **Fit each stream to the audience.** For the seven streams in the
   reference (ads, fan funding, Shopping and affiliate, sponsorships,
   digital products, services, memberships or community outside YouTube),
   rate fit, effort and time to first income, using what this audience
   actually needs.
3. **Pick two to start.** Usually one that works now (affiliate links for
   things already recommended, a service for a skill already shown) and one
   that builds (a product or sponsorship readiness).
4. **Sponsorship readiness.** A one-page media kit: who watches (from
   Studio's audience tab), median views per video over the last 90 days,
   formats offered, past results. Rates: use the reference's method as a
   starting point and say it is a starting point.
5. **RPM reality.** If the channel is monetized, read its RPM from Studio
   `[Studio]`; do not quote niche RPMs as this channel's number. Explain the
   difference between CPM and RPM.

## Rules

- Disclose every paid relationship: YouTube's paid promotion setting plus a
  spoken and written disclosure, and the advertising rules of the
  creator's country (in the US, the FTC's endorsement guides).
- Recommend only what the creator has used or would use; a bad sponsor
  costs more trust than it pays.
- No income claims or guarantees in what you write for them.

## Output: `youtube/monetize.md`

Eligibility status, the stream table, the two chosen streams with first
steps, the media kit draft, and the disclosure checklist.
