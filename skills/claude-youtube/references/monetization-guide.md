# Monetization guide

Ways a channel earns, what each requires, and how to price sponsorships.
Evidence levels as in `algorithm-guide.md`. YouTube's programme terms change
and vary by country; confirm the current numbers in Studio's Earn tab and
YouTube's Help Center before telling a creator they qualify.

## YouTube Partner Program tiers [official]

| tier | requirements (at the time of writing) | unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fan funding (expanded YPP, in countries where offered) | 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months or 3 million public Shorts views in the last 90 days | channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks, YouTube Shopping (where eligible) |
| Full YPP | 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months or 10 million public Shorts views in the last 90 days | the above, plus a share of ad revenue on long form and Shorts, and YouTube Premium revenue |

Also required: following YouTube's monetization policies, living in an
available country, an AdSense account, two-step verification, and no active
Community Guidelines strikes. Shorts views do not count toward the
4,000 watch hours [official].

Revenue shares [official]: creators receive 55 percent of net ad revenue on
long-form videos, and 45 percent of the revenue allocated to them from the
Shorts pool.

## The seven revenue streams

| stream | needs | effort | fits when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Ads (AdSense through YPP) | full YPP | none once set up | consistent long-form views; higher-value audiences |
| 2. Fan funding (memberships, Super Chat, Super Thanks) | expanded YPP | ongoing perks, live streams | a loyal returning audience, community-style channels |
| 3. Shopping and affiliate | affiliate programmes; YouTube Shopping where eligible | low | the creator recommends products already |
| 4. Sponsorships and brand deals | a defined audience; YouTube's paid promotion setting | medium per deal | a clear niche advertisers want to reach |
| 5. Digital products (courses, templates, presets, ebooks) | something worth paying for | high upfront | tutorial, education, authority channels |
| 6. Services (coaching, consulting, freelance) | a skill the videos demonstrate | time-bound | small channels with high-value viewers; often the fastest to first income |
| 7. Memberships and community off YouTube (Patreon, newsletters, paid communities) | a reason to go deeper | ongoing | channels with superfans and depth to offer |

Small channels often earn more from 3, 5 and 6 than from ads [observed].

## Sponsorship rates

There is no official rate card. Common methods [rule of thumb]:

- **Per 1,000 views**: price on the median views a video gets in its first
  30 days (not the subscriber count, not the best video). Quote the method
  and the median `[API]` or `[Studio]`; the CPM-style multiplier is a
  negotiation, and it is usually higher for niches with valuable audiences
  and for dedicated videos than for integrations.
- **Flat fee** for small channels, set by the creator's time and the value
  of the audience; often supplemented by an affiliate commission.
- **Performance deals** (per sign-up or sale) where the brand insists; offer
  a guaranteed minimum.

What changes the rate: dedicated video vs a 60 to 90 second integration,
usage rights (ads using the creator's footage cost more), exclusivity,
deliverables (Shorts, community posts, links), audience country and fit.

Never invent a rate or quote "industry average" numbers as fact. Give the
method, the channel's numbers, and a range the creator chooses.

## Media kit (one page)

Who watches (age, gender, top countries, from Studio's Audience tab
`[Studio]`), median views per long video over 90 days, average view
duration, subscribers, formats offered and their prices, two past results
or testimonials (real ones), contact.

## Disclosure [official]

- Tick "includes paid promotion" in Studio for any video with paid product
  placement, sponsorship or endorsement.
- Say it in the video and in the description, clearly, near the start of
  the integration.
- Follow local law, such as the FTC Endorsement Guides in the US and the
  ASA and CAP rules in the UK.

## Policy risks to monetization [official]

Reused content without meaningful added value, repetitive mass-produced
content, misleading metadata, and inappropriate content for advertisers can
limit or remove monetization. Advertiser-friendly guidelines apply per
video (the yellow icon).
