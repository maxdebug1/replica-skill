# Niche authority channel

Deep expertise in a narrow field: law, medicine, accounting, engineering, a specific software, a craft, a professional skill. Small audience, high value per viewer.

## Formats and lengths

| format | length | role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| deep dive | 15 to 40 min | Hub; trust |
| answer to a precise question | 5 to 12 min | Help; search |
| case study | 10 to 20 min | proof of expertise |
| Shorts | 30 to 60 s | one myth corrected |

Lengths are starting points [rule of thumb]. The right length is the length
the idea needs; check average percentage viewed by length band on the
channel itself.

## Cadence and pillars

- Cadence: weekly or fortnightly; accuracy over volume.
- Pillars: the questions clients or peers ask most, case studies, industry changes.

## Packaging

- Titles: precise, professional, searchable ("Section 179 explained for freelancers"). Credibility beats hype.
- Thumbnails: clean, readable, a professional face or a clear diagram; restrained colour.

## Retention

- Lead with the conclusion, then the reasoning. These viewers value time.
- Credentials in one line, early, only if relevant.
- Examples and worked cases make the expertise concrete.

## Monetization fit

Services and consulting (often the largest), digital products, B2B sponsorships, ads (professional and finance audiences usually attract higher bids). A small channel can earn well here.

See `references/monetization-guide.md` for requirements.

## Pitfalls

- Regulated advice (legal, medical, financial): add clear limits, follow professional rules, and never give individual advice in a video.
- Chasing views with broad topics that dilute the authority.
- Jargon without explanation.

## Metrics to watch first

Leads or enquiries from the channel, search share, returning viewers, average view duration.
