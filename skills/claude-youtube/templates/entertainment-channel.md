# Entertainment channel

Challenges, sketches, stunts, experiments, storytelling, games and group formats. The viewer wants to feel something now.

## Formats and lengths

| format | length | role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| challenge or experiment | 12 to 25 min | Hero; the core |
| recurring series | 10 to 20 min | Hub; a reason to come back |
| Shorts | 15 to 45 s | the best moment, looped; discovery |
| live streams | 1 to 3 h | community, fan funding |

Lengths are starting points [rule of thumb]. The right length is the length
the idea needs; check average percentage viewed by length band on the
channel itself.

## Cadence and pillars

- Cadence: consistent weekly or fortnightly; production values rise with time.
- Pillars: one signature format the channel is known for, plus one or two variations.

## Packaging

- Titles: the premise in a few words, with stakes ("Last to leave the circle wins $10,000"). Browse-first; search barely matters.
- Thumbnails: faces with strong genuine emotion, the premise visible at a glance, high contrast, minimal text.

## Retention

- The premise stated in the first seconds, stakes raised early, escalation every few minutes, the payoff late but promised.
- Fast pacing: cuts, reactions, sound design. Pattern interrupts every 15 to 30 s.
- End on the climax and go straight to the next video.

## Monetization fit

Ads at scale, sponsorships, merchandise, fan funding and live. Entertainment audiences can carry lower ad rates than finance or B2B; volume makes up for it.

See `references/monetization-guide.md` for requirements.

## Pitfalls

- Escalating costs to chase bigger stunts; plan a sustainable format.
- Misleading thumbnails: the payoff must exist.
- Safety and platform policies on dangerous challenges and pranks.

## Metrics to watch first

Impressions growth, CTR at scale, average view duration, shares.
