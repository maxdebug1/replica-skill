# Review channel

Reviews and compares products: tech, cars, cameras, kitchen gear, books, games. The viewer is deciding what to buy.

## Formats and lengths

| format | length | role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| single review | 8 to 15 min | Help; search on the product name |
| comparison (A vs B) | 10 to 20 min | Help; high intent |
| best-of roundup | 12 to 20 min | Help and Browse |
| long-term review | 8 to 15 min | trust builder |
| Shorts | 20 to 45 s | one verdict, one standout feature |

Lengths are starting points [rule of thumb]. The right length is the length
the idea needs; check average percentage viewed by length band on the
channel itself.

## Cadence and pillars

- Cadence: tied to launch cycles in the niche; be early for new products, and keep evergreen comparisons updated.
- Pillars: new releases, comparisons, buying guides, long-term follow-ups.

## Packaging

- Titles: product name first for search; the verdict or the twist for Browse ("iPhone X review: the battery problem").
- Thumbnails: the product large and clean, sometimes the creator's face reacting; one or two words of verdict.

## Retention

- Give the verdict early (who it is for), then the evidence. Viewers who only want the answer can leave happy; the rest stay for proof.
- Show testing, not spec sheets.
- Chapters per criterion (battery, camera, price).

## Monetization fit

Affiliate (often the main income), sponsorships, ads (consumer tech and cars attract good demand).

See `references/monetization-guide.md` for requirements.

## Pitfalls

- Disclosure of free products, loans and paid reviews, every time.
- Independence: a review channel lives on trust; turn down deals that require approval of the verdict.
- Being late to launches; and outdated "best of" lists.

## Metrics to watch first

Search share, affiliate click-through, average view duration, comments asking for comparisons.
